About

Lintel Loaderv1.2 “Report”

A single-page design aid for masonry lintels and the piers either side, in the style of Frame Pusher. Everything runs in your browser; your design is kept in this browser only.

What it does

It finds the load on the lintel from the masonry above, using either a 60° or 45° arching triangle or the full masonry height, together with floors and dispersed point loads. It then checks steel, reinforced-concrete or proprietary lintels. For the walls either side it checks bearing, axial capacity, arch thrust and the Approved Document A pier rules. See Method for the full set of assumptions.

Lintel Loader · method

A design aid for a lintel over an opening in a single leaf of loadbearing masonry, and for the masonry piers either side. Units: kN, m (inputs in mm). Run each leaf of a cavity wall separately with the loads that leaf carries.

Loads on the lintel

  • Arching. Where the masonry above can arch, the lintel carries only the masonry inside a load triangle (60° to the horizontal by default, as BS 5977-1) plus loads applied within a loading zone of height 1.1 × clear span. Loads above the zone are carried by the arch to the piers. In Auto mode arching is taken only when: the masonry above is at least the triangle height + 150 mm, there are no openings or movement joints in the zone, neither side is a free wall end, and both piers resist the arch thrust.
  • No arching. All masonry up to the height entered and every floor are carried over the full clear span.
  • Floors are line loads (kN/m² × tributary width). Within the zone they act over the full span; optionally, those inside the triangle can act only over the triangle chord at floor level.
  • Point loads spread at 60° to the horizontal through the masonry (EC6 6.1.3) onto the lintel. The part of the spread that falls beyond a jamb goes into that pier.
  • Lintel span = clear span + one bearing length (centre to centre of bearings). Self-weight is included.

Lintel checks

  • Steel (EN 1993-1-1): section classification, Mc,Rd (Wpl or Wel), Vpl,Rd, and SLS deflection under G + Q. If the flange is not restrained, LTB uses Mcr with C1 = 1.13 and rolled-section curves b/c. Properties come from the nominal dimensions with root radii ignored, which is slightly conservative.
  • Reinforced concrete (EN 1992-1-1): rectangular stress block bending, VRd,c without links, span/effective-depth (7.4.2) and minimum steel. The deflection diagram uses a rough long-term cracked stiffness (Ecm/3, 0.5 Ig) and is indicative only.
  • Proprietary lintels: the equivalent UDL, max(8M/L, 2Rmax) under unfactored loads, is compared with the maker's safe working load. Use the maker's table for the actual length, leaf ratio and load case.

Walls either side (piers)

  • Bearing (EC6 6.1.3): NEd ≤ β Ab fd, with a1 = 0 at the jamb. The effective length lefm spreads at 60° into the pier only, so it is capped by the pier width. Ab/Aef ≤ 0.45, and β = 1 for units that are not Group 1.
  • Axial (EC6 6.1.2 / Annex G): the pier is a column b × t with hef = ρn h. It carries the lintel reaction, the masonry and floors over the pier, the share of arched loads, and any point-load spread beyond the jamb. The check is made at bearing level (Φi) and at mid-height (Φm, with em ≈ einit + 0.6 etop). The small-area factor (0.7 + 3A) applies when A < 0.1 m².
  • Arch thrust: T = WarchL/(8z), with z = 0.8 × min(triangle height, masonry height). It is resisted by bed-joint shear in the pier, fvk = fvk0 + 0.4σd ≤ 0.065fb, with σd from permanent load only.
  • Approved Document A (England, Diagram 14, advisory): no opening wider than 3 m, total openings ≤ 2L/3, a return pier of at least max(665 mm, W/X), and a pier between openings of at least the larger opening / X. A free wall end falls outside the guidance.

Masonry properties

fk = K fb0.7 fm0.3, with fm ≤ min(20, 2fb). fvk0 comes from EC6 Table 3.4. The library values are typical and indicative; replace them with the manufacturer's declared normalised strength and density.

Limits

This is a design aid, not a substitute for engineering judgement. It does not check lateral (wind) loading on the piers, concentrated loads on the lintel's bottom flange or toe, cavity-tray or thermal details, or construction-stage (green masonry) loading, except through the LTB option. All results should be verified by a competent engineer.
